Toward Independent Journalistic Audits of AI Chatbot Responses

Friday, October 2, 2026; 3:30–5:00 PM CT · Northwestern University

Overview

This workshop will introduce journalists to AI auditing with a hands-on tutorial that allows participants to run their own audits of AI chatbots. Participants will share how they currently investigate (or are interested in investigating) AI-generated information on critical social and political topics, and identify questions relevant to their reporting. Then, facilitators will provide a demonstration and overview of longitudinal AI auditing—the process of investigating AI chatbots by asking the same set of questions on a repeating schedule, revealing how the chatbots respond and change over time. With support from facilitators, participants will use Penn’s Open Auditor System to conduct their own audits on topics of personal interest. No coding or technical background is needed.

Agenda

This workshop will run for 90 minutes, from 3:30–5:00 PM CT.

  1. 3:30 – 3:40 PM · Introduction

    The facilitators will share the motivation behind this project, highlighting the importance of audits for examining how models respond on critical social and political topics, as users increasingly rely on chatbots for information about them. We will look at examples of reporting that audits have made possible. These include stories from the BBC, Columbia Journalism Review, and OpenDemocracy. We will also set out what an audit can and cannot show.

  2. 3:40 – 3:50 PM · Pre-discussion

    We will invite brief reflections from participants about how they have been covering AI chatbots (or would love to cover chatbots) and the challenges or limitations they face in conducting independent investigations.

  3. 3:50 – 4:00 PM · Demonstration

    The facilitators will walk through an example audit that has been running for a few weeks prior to the workshop. The demonstration will guide participants through how AI model responses have evolved over time, including variation in sources cited by the chatbot. We then set up a new audit from scratch so participants can see how the Open Auditor tool works.

  4. 4:00 – 4:15 PM · Q&A + Group Discussion of Possible Use Cases

    After the demonstration, we will address any questions about the tool and its functionality and discuss how the platform might support the questions and investigations identified earlier. Each participant will be invited to share their reflection as well as one potential use case(s) based on their domain expertise.

  5. 4:15 – 4:40 PM · Run Your Own Audit

    Working individually or in small groups, participants will choose a question, write a set of queries, and launch their own audit on the Community AI Auditing Platform. The facilitators will be available to support. Further we will provide pre-prepared example query sets, on topics such as abortion and immigration, that participants can opt to use or adapt to their needs.

  6. 4:40 – 5:00 PM · Feedback and Discussion

    Before we close, we will have an open discussion about the participant experience running an AI audit and where the tool might fit into participants’ work and reporting. Since the tool is still in development, we welcome participant experience and feedback on the user interface and function—feedback which will directly inform the tool’s development. Was the process intuitive on the first try? Did the results meet participants’ expectations? Did participants feel like they learned something because of this auditing process that they might not have seen otherwise? We are particularly keen to know how we can improve the tool to better address the investigative needs of journalists.
